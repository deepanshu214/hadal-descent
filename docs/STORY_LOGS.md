```
SURVEY TEAM ALPHA — FIELD REPORT — DAY 1

Initial trench survey complete. Geology consistent with established

mineral deposits confirmed at 4,200m along the eastern fault wall.

Of note: unusual acoustic returns at 6,800m depth. Sonar shows a large
cavity beneath the trench floor — approximately 200m across, 80m tall.

Dr. Voss has requested drill access to the cavity. Commander Webb has

Nothing else to report. Standard salvage environment.
```

### LOG 1-05: Engineering Assessment
**Found in:** Tier 1 — Maintenance Tunnel
**Type:** Technical report
```
ENGINEERING ASSESSMENT — DRILL TARGET ALPHA

Substrate composition at target depth (6,800m below seabed):
  - Upper layer: basaltic crust, standard
  - Mid layer: serpentinized peridotite, some unusual mineral veins
  - Lower layer: [INSUFFICIENT DATA — requires core sample]

The cavity itself is sealed. Whatever formed it, the surrounding rock
has fused into a shell approximately 12m thick. This is not consistent
with any known geological process. Magma chambers don't seal themselves.

Recommendation: Proceed with caution. The D-7 can penetrate the shell,
but if the cavity is pressurized, breach could cause a localized
seismic event.

— R. Okafor, Chief Engineer
```

### LOG 1-08: Chief Engineer — Personal Note
**Found in:** Tier 1 — Engineering Workshop

```
[HANDWRITTEN — SLIGHTLY SHAKY]

Sarah,

If you're reading this, I left it in my toolkit where I always
leave your letters. I know you worry.

Things are fine here. Mostly. The drill is running 18 hours a day

I'll write again when I can. Don't worry about me.

Love,
Remi
```

### LOG 2-02: Dr. Voss — Personal Journal, Entry 7
**Found in:** Tier 2 — Sealed Lab Corridor
**Type:** Audio log — rushed, excited
```
[AUDIO — FEMALE VOICE, RAPID, BREATHLESS]

"It's alive. It's alive and it's — God, I don't even know how to
classify it. It's not a single organism. It's a colony. Millions
of interconnected cells forming a distributed biological network
that communicates through pressure differentials and acoustic
wave patterns.

It's been living in that cavity, in complete darkness, at 150
degrees Celsius, for almost two billion years. It predates
complex life on the surface by a billion years.

The colony samples we extracted are responding to stimuli. They
react to sound. They react to pressure changes. And they're...
learning. The response patterns are becoming more complex with
each test cycle.

I've requested we halt extraction until we understand what we're
dealing with. Meridian denied the request within minutes.

They want more samples. Larger samples.

I have a very bad feeling about this."
```

### LOG 2-03: Crew Personal — Audio Diary
**Found in:** Tier 2 — Crew Quarters (damaged)
**Type:** Audio log — anonymous crew member
```
[AUDIO — MALE VOICE, WHISPERING]

"Day 11. Something's wrong with the station.

The pressure alarms keep going off on Deck D — short bursts, then
silence. Engineering says it's sensor drift from the drill thermal
output. But I've worked pressure systems for fifteen years and
sensor drift doesn't come in rhythmic patterns.

It's like something is... testing the walls. Pushing against them,
then stopping. Pushing, then stopping.

And the sounds. At night, through the bulkheads, I can hear this
low frequency hum. Not mechanical. It's organic. It rises and falls
like breathing.

I asked to be rotated out on the next supply sub. Morrow — the
security guy — said no one leaves until the project is complete.

What project? We were told this was geological survey."
```

### LOG 2-06: Chief Engineer — Final Letter
**Found in:** Tier 2 — Hidden behind a panel (secret)
**Type:** Handwritten note
```
[HANDWRITTEN — VERY SHAKY]

Sarah,

I don't think I'm going to be able to send any more letters.

The station is failing. Not breaking down — being taken apart.
The pressure seals on Deck D are gone. The bio-matter from the
drill shaft has reached containment. It went through the
containment walls like they weren't there.

It doesn't move like a liquid or a solid. It moves like it
knows where it's going. Like it has a plan.

Morrow sealed the blast doors between Decks C and D. He says
it's containment. But I looked at the lock codes. They're not
keeping it in.

They're keeping us out.

Whatever Meridian wanted from that cavity, I think they're
about to get it. And I think it's going to cost all of us.

I love you. I'm sorry.

Remi
```

### LOG 2-07: Dr. Voss — Personal Journal, Entry 12
**Found in:** Tier 2 — Voss's Quarters (ransacked)
**Type:** Audio log — calm, resigned
```
[AUDIO — FEMALE VOICE, EERILY CALM]

"I've been confined to quarters for three days. It doesn't matter.

I can hear the colony through the walls now. Not with my ears —
with my bones. It communicates at frequencies below human hearing,
but at this range, you feel it in your teeth, in your sternum.

I've been analyzing the patterns. It's not random. It's language.
Or something close to language. Pressure sequences that encode
information. It's been mapping the station's architecture by
sending pressure pulses through the corridors and listening to
the echoes.

Echolocation. On a structural scale.

It knows the layout of this facility better than we do. It knows
where every sealed door is. Every weak point. Every person.

Morrow thinks the blast doors will hold. Morrow is wrong.

I've hidden my research data in an encrypted drive in the sample
vault on Deck C. If anyone finds this recording: the encryption
key is in log entry 4-03. Find the drive. Get it to the surface.
The world needs to know what Meridian did here.

And what we woke up."
```
